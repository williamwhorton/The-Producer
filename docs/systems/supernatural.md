# Supernatural Entities and Contestant Resistance

## Core premise [CONFIRMED — #23]
The producer cultivates psychic conditions that probabilistically enable entities to emerge. All genuine supernatural activity originates from an existing autonomous entity. Energy is not consumed by manifestations. Belief facilitates emergence, imprints shape entities, and anchoring enables persistence independently of original causes. House-wide and localized conditions coexist. Contestant ghosts are possible, not guaranteed or required. See [approved emergence design](../issue-23-supernatural-escalation.md).

## Separation from producer-installed effects [CONFIRMED boundary]

The producer begins with mundane preparation and interventions such as manipulated household objects, hidden sound effects, lights or window movements. Effects require a credible prepared or otherwise authorized physical means; they **do not** turn the producer into an omnipotent ghost. Unseen quick staging during paused curfew is approved; holographic ghost illusions remain proposed. Once true supernatural activity develops, its own rules and autonomy matter independently of the producer's preinstalled devices. See [ADR-0006](../decisions/ADR-0006-producer-preparation-and-practical-effects.md) and Issue #6 for unresolved supernatural limits.

## Entity model [PROPOSED]
Traits such as aggression, territoriality, fixation, volatility, responsiveness and independence. A drive/goal selection mechanism should produce legible but not perfectly predictable behavior. Examples are poltergeist, apparition and place-bound spirit; taxonomy not final.

## Contestant counterplay [CONFIRMED]
Contestants can detect and avoid supernatural threats, gather information, develop protections, attempt exorcism/banishment, and sometimes permanently neutralize entities. Cooperative investigation and rituals should be possible in the simulation, not just authored cutscenes.

## Producer conflict [PROPOSED]
Producer may need to preserve an entity from contestant banishment or prevent autonomous escalation from causing mass departures. Direct entity control is not approved. An unsuccessful ritual might change the situation, but this is not yet an approved rule.

## Testing contracts
- Entities act without player commands in seeded scenarios.
- Same initial seed and actions reproduce event outcomes.
- Entity choices can be traced to goals/constraints for QA.
- Contestant countermeasures can succeed under defined conditions.
- Entity can disrupt a carefully planned production strategy.
- Entities are not required to behave strategically on behalf of the producer.

## Open decisions
Specific drives, capabilities, countermeasures and extinction conditions (#6); numerical emergence and anchoring rules; persistence evaluation (#7). Keep the first playable implementation small.
